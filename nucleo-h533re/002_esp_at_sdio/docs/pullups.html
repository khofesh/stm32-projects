The pull-up network, wired correctly

Each resistor sits between 3V3 and its signal, never in line with it. The signal wire from the STM32 and the wire to the ESP32 meet at the resistor's lower lead — that junction is the net. Get that node wrong and the host can never pull the line down.

Correct topology

Five 10 kΩ resistors, one per net: CMD and D0–D3. CK gets none — it is driven by the host only, and never released.

The node, close up

One net, three conductors, one point. This is the whole fix: the STM32 wire and the ESP32 wire land on the resistor's lower lead — the side away from 3V3.

What the board is doing instead

The host measures all five nets as a near-zero-ohm path to 3.3 V. Two wiring mistakes produce that, and both look correct at a glance.

On a breadboard

This is where the second failure mode comes from. The five-hole column is the node; the long red strip beside it is the supply rail. They are not the same thing.

Two strapping consequences of these pull-ups, unchanged by the rework: GPIO2 (D0) must be held low while flashing the ESP32 over serial, and GPIO12 (D2) needs the 3.3 V flash-voltage eFuse already burned on this module.

Confirming the rework

The host tests this at every boot before it clocks the bus. Power up with the STM32 console open — a good network reads: 

host ready, start initializing slave...
E sdio_driver: pin diag: control CTRL(PA5) drive0=ok drive1=ok
E sdio_driver: pin diag: pull-up census (expect 'high' on CMD and D0-D3)
E sdio_driver:   CMD(PB2): high  (something pulls this net up)
E sdio_driver:   D0(PB13): high  (something pulls this net up)
E sdio_driver:   D1(PC9): high  (something pulls this net up)
E sdio_driver:   D2(PC10): high  (something pulls this net up)
E sdio_driver:   D3(PC11): high  (something pulls this net up)
E sdio_driver: pin diag: short matrix
E sdio_driver: pin diag: wiring looks sane from the host side
  • Every net still reads high at idle — that is the pull-up doing its job.
  • The short matrix stays silent. Any driven low but reads high line means that net is still tied to the rail.
  • Once the matrix is clean, enumeration proceeds and CMD5 should return a response instead of err=0x00000004.
